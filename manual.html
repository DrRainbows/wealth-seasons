A lifetime investment game, built from a pattern language of 100+ years of investing evidence. Not advice. Not a forecast. A teaching instrument with score, achievements, and scenarios.

What makes it a game (not just a calculator)

  • A single number to chase — the Stewardship Score, live in the status bar. Composed of pattern adherence + achievements + survival + inflation-adjusted final wealth.
  • Two ghosts live your life beside you — from year one, two companion sims advance through the identical world (same markets, same life events, same lifespan): one on published mainstream-average behavior, one on the pattern language. Their lines are on your chart the whole game.
  • 7 scenarios with explicit goals — Survive 1929, Joseph's Test, House Hacker, Late Starter, Family of Four, FIRE by 50, Fresh Start.
  • 25+ achievements pop as toasts mid-game; "big" milestones pause with a modal.
  • Uncertainty — random historical-start drops you into 1928-1965 and you live through actual market history without knowing the year.
  • Tension — selling into a -25% year is a -3 pattern hit; holding through it is +2. Joseph reserves only matter if you also enable Cycle Rhythm.
  • Replayability — same seed reproduces a life. Try different strategies against identical markets.

1. What this game is

You play a single human life: you start somewhere between age 22 and 70, you receive wages each year, you make a small number of decisions, and a real or stochastic market unfolds around you. The engine runs year by year. Each year applies returns, fires life events, computes taxes to the dollar, and scores how closely your behavior matches a set of patterns observed in high-performing investors going back to 1928.

Two things matter:

  1. Outcome — your inflation-adjusted net worth, whether you ran out of money, what you transferred to heirs.
  2. Pattern adherence — how often you behaved like the disciplined operators the engine is calibrated against.

The game ends when you die (probabilistic SSA-style mortality past 80, certain at 105). You then receive an archetype label and a decade-by-decade summary.

3. The annual cycle

In order, every year:

  1. Allocation changes execute as trades at the start of the year. New weights apply to every account immediately; in the taxable account, reducing risk sleeves sells appreciated shares and realizes LTCG taxed this year.
  2. Wages received (taxed on the same figure). Growth (real + inflation) applies at year-end.
  3. Spending and home costs deducted from Cash/Emergency. Homes carry mortgage P&I, ~1% maintenance, ~0.8% property tax.
  4. Investment property rents (LTR or STR), operating expenses, mortgage interest, and 27.5-yr depreciation are computed; actual cash flow lands in your cash account. Values appreciate via FHFA housing index.
  5. Farmland cash rent (~3%, credited to cash) + appreciation (USDA NASS).
  6. Business cash flow (credited to cash) grows or shocks (4%/yr major-loss risk; otherwise N(10%, 30%)).
  7. Liquid accounts grow sleeve by sleeve — and weights drift: a portfolio left alone becomes what its winners make it. Annual rebalancing is what resets it to your policy.
  8. RMD taken if at/past start age (73 if born 1951-59, 75 if born 1960+).
  9. Social Security paid with COLA if claimed. Auto-claims at 70. Benefit computed from your actual career earnings (top 35 years, CPI-indexed bend points).
  10. Withdrawals follow tax-efficient order: cash → taxable (gains from actual tracked basis) → traditional → Roth.
  11. Taxes computed to the dollar: federal brackets, capital gains stacked, NIIT, Alabama state, IRMAA at 65+, FICA on wages. Overdrawn cash triggers forced liquidation of investments (taxed next year).
  12. Roth conversion if requested — taxed as ordinary income now.
  13. Annual rebalance if enabled — resets drifted weights to policy.
  14. Joseph reserve: in good years a slice of cash is diverted to the reserve.
  15. Cycle Rhythm: if enabled and last year was -15%+, half the reserve auto-deploys to equities.
  16. Lifestyle absorption: unassigned cash above your cash target partially drifts into consumption during working years (see the money model below). Logged every year.
  17. Patterns scored.
  18. Life events roll: promotion, layoff, marriage, child, health, inheritance, vacancy, business shock. Your ghosts receive the same events.
  19. Mortality roll.

The money model: assigned vs unassigned dollars

Money you deliberately assign is money you keep. Unassigned money drifts away. Every dollar of income lands in Cash/Emergency. Dollars you direct — contributions, taxable investing, reserves, a down-payment fund (raise your cash target), giving, purchases — go where you sent them. Cash above the cash target has no assignment, and roughly half the excess is absorbed into lifestyle each working year. The final report totals it, per decade, next to your taxes.

This is the published record of average behavior, not a punishment: the U.S. personal saving rate runs ~4-5% of disposable income (BEA, 2024-25), while 401(k) participants who automate defer 7.7% on average (Vanguard How America Saves 2025) — and keep it, because it never touches checking. Wage growth defaults to 1% real above inflation (long-run SSA Average Wage Index); promotions arrive as life events on top. All "Real" figures deflate by the simulation's actual cumulative CPI.

5. The pattern language

Each pattern declares what it watches, the problem it solves, what it asks of you, and how the engine scores it each year. Patterns are coupled — activating one usually reinforces or constrains others.

Layer 0 — Human Capital

Human Capital Maximizer

Scores when your real wage growth beats inflation by 2-4%. Your future earnings are your largest asset early — skill, network, disability insurance.

Layer 1 — Foundational (always active)

Compound Early and Often

+2/year for ≥15% savings rate under 35. Time, not return, dominates early.

Own the Market Broadly

+1 when core sleeve ≥50% equity at low fee. Low-turnover indexing.

Diversify Across Seasons

+1 for ≥3 sleeves; +1 for real assets; -1 for >95% equities past 55.

Avoid Permanent Loss

+1 for ≥3 months cash; -1 for <1 month; -2 for panic-selling in a -15% year.

Stay the Course

+2 for holding through a -15% year; -3 for cutting equities >5% in a -30% year.

Continuous Rebalancing

+1 when rebalanceAnnually is on.

Circle of Competence

-1/yr per asset class owned outside your competence.

Behavioral Fortress

+1 for stable written policy; -1 for >25% allocation churn.

Biblical Stewardship (meta)

Composite: diligence + prudence + diversification + generosity. Up to +2/year.

Layer 2 — Asset & Risk Engines

Real Asset Income Engine

+1/year for any rental, farm, or primary home.

Residence as Dual-Use Capital

+1 for moderate LTV; -1 if primary >60% NW past 40.

Serial §121 Harvester

+2 in the year you take a §121 exclusion; -1 if ≥4 cycles pre-55.

Judicious Leverage

+1 for LTV <0.75; -1 for >0.85.

Cycle-Aware Allocation

Tilts within Diversify.

Time in the Market

Implicit — panic-sells lose; sustained holds compound.

Inflation Protection Engine

+2 in >5% inflation year with ≥20% real assets/metals.

Precious Metals Fortress

+1 for 5-15% metals; -1 for >30%.

Joseph's Cycle-Reserve

+1 for ≥1 year of spending in reserve; +1 in >15% years with Joseph rate ≥10%.

Cycle Rhythm Navigator

+2 in any year you deployed reserves into a >10% correction.

Nehemiah's Rebuild

+1 for sustained 3+ year project (business or §121 home).

Layer 3 — Tax & Account Optimization

Tax-Advantaged Maximizer

+2 when sheltered contributions ≥2× taxable and >$5k.

Tax Shelter Allocation

Directs where every other pattern's assets live.

Pre-Tax Account Bridge

+2 in gap years (retired, 60+, pre-RMD) when converting >$5k.

Charitable Multiplier

+1 for >5% wage giving; +2 for QCD >$5k post-70.

Layer 4 — Retirement Distribution

Sequence-of-Returns Shield

+1 for 2-6 years of cash; -1 for <1.

SS Longevity Optimizer

+2 for 70+; +1 for FRA+; -1 for 62.

Retirement Spending Curve

+1 for smile/declining (empirical "go-go / slow-go / no-go").

Layer 5 — Legacy

Legacy & Step-Up Basis

+1 past 70 for holding >$250k taxable gains untouched.

6. How patterns couple (the generative rules)

Examples the engine encodes:

  • Compound Early + Tax Shelter → Roth-heavy contributions early amplify both.
  • Real Asset + Judicious Leverage + Avoid Permanent Loss → buying a property boosts the first two and tightens the third (more cash reserves needed).
  • §121 Harvester + Behavioral Fortress → tax-free flips are a win but lifestyle-creep risk — engine penalizes >4 cycles pre-55.
  • Pre-Tax Bridge + SS Optimizer → converting in gap years lowers future RMDs and SS taxation in your 70s.
  • Joseph Reserve + Cycle Rhythm → storing in bulls enables deploying in bears. Either alone is incomplete.
  • Curved Spending + Sequence Shield → front-loaded spending raises sequence risk; buckets exist to absorb it.
  • Inflation Protection + Real Asset + Metals → three angles on the same defense. Engine wants at least one active >5% inflation.
  • Biblical Stewardship (meta) → sits over all four legs (diligence, prudence, diversification, generosity).

7. What decisions you make each year

Working years

  • Contributions to Roth 401(k), Trad 401(k), Roth IRA, Trad IRA, HSA, Taxable. 2026 limits: 401(k) $23,500 ($31k catch-up at 50+); IRA $7,500 ($8,500 catch-up); HSA $4,300.
  • Allocation — stocks / bonds / cash / REIT / metals. Must sum to 100%.
  • Major moves: Buy primary home, Buy vacation home, Buy rental ($150k), Buy STR ($250k), Buy farmland ($80k cash), Start business ($50k). Once you own a primary, the button changes to Remodel and after 2 years, Sell primary (§121) appears.
  • Stewardship & strategy: Joseph reserve rate (0–25%), charitable giving this year, Cycle Rhythm toggle.

Retirement

  • Same allocation controls.
  • Spending curve: Flat / Smile / Declining.
  • Roth conversion this year ($) — best in gap years.
  • Claim SS if not yet claimed; auto-claims at 70.
  • QCD input once you reach age 70.

8. The Stewardship Score

A single composite number, visible at all times in the status bar. Roughly 0–1000 over a full life.

Stewardship Score = pattern_net
                  + 15 × achievements
                  + survival_bonus   (+100 if positive NW at death)
                  + wealth_bonus     (up to +200 at $10M real)
                  − shortfall_penalty (−150 if ever ran out)

Not a substitute for any individual metric — it's a deliberately blunt single-number summary so runs can be compared.

10. The three ghosts (live comparison)

Two companion lives are created the moment yours begins and advance every time you do — through the identical world: the same recorded market returns, the same promotions, layoffs, marriage, children, health costs and inheritances (replayed with the same magnitudes), and exactly your lifespan.

  • Mainstream Autopilot — verified published averages: 7.7% deferral (Vanguard How America Saves 2025), Vanguard target-date glide path, Social Security at FRA (67). No conversions, no reserves, no real assets.
  • Pattern-Disciplined — the full pattern-language autopilot: 15% savings, Roth-first early, gap-year conversions, SS at 70.
  • You — whatever you actually did.

Both ghosts retire at 65 so retirement timing is never the confound. The final report shows each life's ending wealth, real wealth, lifetime taxes, and lifetime lifestyle absorption — the gap between Mainstream and Disciplined in the same life is the number the game exists to surface.

This is the central game-vs-simulation distinction: you're not playing against an abstract "good investor", you're playing against the disciplined pattern-default with full knowledge of your market and your life events.

12. The tax system in detail

The engine computes federal + Alabama tax to the dollar each year using 2026 brackets.

  • Ordinary brackets: 10 / 12 / 22 / 24 / 32 / 35 / 37%.
  • Standard deduction: $15,800 single, $31,600 MFJ; +$2,050 / $1,650 senior bonus at 65+.
  • LTCG & qualified dividends: 0 / 15 / 20%. 0% bracket ends at $49,000 single / $98,000 MFJ.
  • Collectibles (physical metals): 28% LTCG.
  • NIIT: 3.8% on net investment income above $200k single / $250k MFJ MAGI.
  • SS taxation: provisional income above $25k/$32k → 50% taxable; above $34k/$44k → 85%.
  • IRMAA: Medicare surcharges past $106k/$212k MAGI at 65+.
  • FICA: 7.65% on wages up to $176,100, +Medicare add-ons above $200k/$250k.
  • Section 121: $250k single / $500k MFJ excluded on primary home gain, every 2 years.
  • QCD: up to $108k/yr from IRA to charity, ages 70.5+, satisfies RMD without income tax.
  • Alabama: 2 / 4 / 5% brackets, $3k / $8.5k standard deduction, exempts SS & most retirement income, exempts bullion sales.
  • Above-the-line: Trad 401(k), Trad IRA, HSA.
  • Itemized vs standard: engine takes the larger of (charitable + mortgage int. cap + SALT $10k) vs std.

13. Quick strategy primer

Early career (22-35)

  • Push Roth 401(k) + Roth IRA + HSA to limit. Low bracket — pay now, grow forever tax-free.
  • Allocation 80-90% stocks. Maybe 5% metals as insurance.
  • Buy a primary home only if you'll live there 3+ years.
  • Build Circle of Competence in one extra area before owning that asset class.

Mid-career (35-55)

  • Mix Trad and Roth as bracket climbs into 22-24%.
  • Add one rental, farmland, or business at edge of competence.
  • Glide equity allocation down ~1%/yr. By 55 ≈ 70 / 20 / 5 / 5.
  • Turn on Joseph reserve 10-15%. Turn on Cycle Rhythm.
  • Consider §121 cycle every 3-4 years.

Pre-retirement (55-65)

  • Cash bucket = 2-3 years of spending. Sequence shield active.
  • Plan Roth conversion ladder for the gap years before SS & RMDs.
  • Delay SS — the 8%/yr delay credit from 67 to 70 is risk-free 8%.

Early retirement (65-75)

  • Spending curve: Smile if your taste is "experiences early, calm later."
  • Withdraw taxable first (step-up planning), then traditional, then Roth last.
  • Continue Roth conversions until RMD start.

Late retirement (75+)

  • QCDs from IRA satisfy RMDs without ordinary income.
  • Don't sell appreciated taxable — heirs get the step-up.
  • Real-asset cash flow is your inflation-protected income floor.

15. Limitations (honest about these)

  • One household, one earner.
  • Only AL state-tax modeled in detail; CA / TX / FL are placeholders.
  • Property values use a national index; no local market.
  • Health is a single probabilistic shock; no Long-Term Care modeling.
  • Business modeled as one shock-prone equity stake; no acquisition / exit dynamics.
  • Federal brackets are 2026 estimates and don't re-baseline across decades.
  • Mortgage rates are entered manually; no dynamic rate environment.
  • Inheritance is a probabilistic event; no estate-transfer modeling of your assets.

The simulation is illustrative. Past patterns don't predict future results. Talk to a fiduciary and CPA before acting on any insight.
